EnerLint

Can a small, independent check tell us whether exported model values obey their declared mass and energy balances?

A Python package for scope-limited physical consistency checks. Initial evidence is synthetic and hand-constructed—not independent validation or certification. It even keeps one deliberately undetected error visible.

02 / How I work

Keep the method inspectable.

The workbench is a place to publish both answers and the route taken to reach them. Not every experiment will become software, and not every result will be positive.

01 — ASK

Start with a real question.

State the physical problem, the quantities involved and what would count as a useful answer.

02 — TEST

Show the calculation.

Keep inputs, units, methods and tests visible. Use analytical references or independent evidence when the question requires them.

03 — SHARE

Publish the edges.

Document what passed, what failed, what was not checked and what someone else would need to reproduce the work.

05 / The person behind the work

One workbench.
Many questions.

I'm Abhijith Sivaprasadan, an engineer working across sustainable energy engineering, thermodynamics and computational modelling.

Open Energy Methods gives my projects a public place of their own. It is where I can publish code and numerical models, explain how experiments were set up, acknowledge uncertainty and keep the inconvenient results in the record.

“A finished run is the beginning of a technical question, not always the end of one.”

OEM is not a registered company, a university research group or a certification body. There is no requirement for it to become any of those. For now the objective is simply to make worthwhile engineering work open, understandable and reproducible.
